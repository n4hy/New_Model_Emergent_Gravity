\begin{abstract}
\noindent
This note contains only \pP{} statements.

Jacobson 1995, under the hypotheses he states---entropy proportional
to area, $\delta Q=T\,dS$, Unruh temperature, heat as boost-energy
flux, local Rindler horizons instantaneously stationary, and
$\nabla^a T_{ab}=0$---implies the Einstein equation with an
\emph{undetermined} cosmological constant and with no torsion
\cite{Jacobson1995}. The null-quadratic lemma in that argument is
an identity and is re-derived here. \pP{}

Jacobson 2016 states that for conformal fields the maximal vacuum
entanglement hypothesis is equivalent to Einstein at first order,
and that for nonconformal fields the same conclusion holds
\emph{modulo a conjecture} about $\delta\langle K\rangle$
\cite{Jacobson2016}. The installed Standard Model is not a CFT
($b_3=-7$). The conformal half therefore does not apply to NSM
matter. The nonconformal half is not \pP{}, by Jacobson's own
words. \pP{}

Paper~IX's cosmological bar is FGHMV-grade Einstein$+\Lambda$
(metric). Torsion is not on that bar. Jacobson 1995 produces
Einstein with free $\Lambda$, which is not a derived first-law sign
and is not an FGHMV equivalence. Jacobson 2016 does not become
\pP{} for the SM. Therefore Jacobson is not a \pP{} substitute
for Q2.
\end{abstract}

\section{The 1995 implication}

Jacobson assumes $dS=\eta\,\delta\mathcal{A}$,
$T=\hbar\kappa/2\pi$, $\delta Q=\int T_{ab}\chi^a\,d\Sigma^b$ on a
local Rindler horizon with $\theta=\sigma=0$ at the bifurcation
surface, and $\nabla^a T_{ab}=0$. Raychaudhuri then gives
$\theta=-\lambda R_{ab}k^ak^b$ near the surface, and
$\delta Q=T\,dS$ forces
\begin{equation}
T_{ab}k^ak^b
=
\frac{\hbar\eta}{2\pi}\,R_{ab}k^ak^b
\qquad\text{for every null }k.
\label{eq:null}
\end{equation}
The algebraic content of \eqref{eq:null} is the following lemma.

\begin{lemma}[null quadratic form]
\label{lem:null}
Let $S_{ab}$ be symmetric on Lorentzian $\mathbb{R}^{1,3}$. If
$S_{ab}k^ak^b=0$ for every null $k$, then $S_{ab}=f\,\eta_{ab}$.
\pP{}
\end{lemma}

\emph{Proof.} Mostly minus, $k=(1,n)$ with $n\cdot n=1$. Then
$S_{00}+2S_{0i}n^i+S_{ij}n^in^j=0$ for every unit $n$. The odd
part forces $S_{0i}=0$. Polarization on
$\{e_i,(e_i+e_j)/\sqrt{2}\}$ is a six-by-seven linear system
whose kernel is one-dimensional and is spanned by
$S_{00}=-\lambda$, $S_{ij}=\lambda\delta_{ij}$. That is
$S=\lambda\eta$. The auditor solves that system; the kernel is
exactly $(-1,1,1,1,0,0,0)$ when $S_{00}$ is normalized to $-1$.

\begin{theorem}[Jacobson 1995, algebra isolated]
\label{thm:1995}
Lemma~\ref{lem:null} and $\nabla^a T_{ab}=0$ together with the
contracted Bianchi identity convert \eqref{eq:null} into
\begin{equation}
R_{ab}-\tfrac12 R\,g_{ab}+\Lambda g_{ab}
=
\frac{2\pi}{\hbar\eta}\,T_{ab},
\qquad
G=\frac{1}{4\hbar\eta},
\label{eq:ein}
\end{equation}
with $\Lambda$ an arbitrary constant. \pP{}
\end{theorem}

Jacobson writes that $\Lambda$ ``remains as enigmatic as ever''
\cite{Jacobson1995}. The constant is not fixed by $\delta Q=T\,dS$.
If $\nabla^a T_{ab}=0$ is dropped, $f$ in $T_{ab}=\alpha(R_{ab}+fg_{ab})$
need not be $-R/2+\Lambda$. That mutation is why conservation is
load-bearing.

\begin{theorem}[no Einstein--Cartan, no Hehl--Datta]
\label{thm:nohd}
The 1995 input list is $(g,R_{ab},T_{ab},k)$. It does not contain
an independent connection, a spin tensor, or $J_5$. The output
\eqref{eq:ein} is Einstein, not Einstein--Cartan, and it does not
contain $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{HD}}=-(3\kappa/16)J_5\cdot J_5$. \pP{}
\end{theorem}

\section{The 2016 split, in Jacobson's words}

Jacobson 2016, abstract and \S IV: for first-order conformal
fields the Einstein equation holds if and only if the maximal
vacuum entanglement hypothesis holds; ``for nonconformal fields,
the same conclusion follows modulo a conjecture about the
variation of entanglement entropy.'' He then writes, verbatim,
that he will ``conjecture---and we shall assume'' that
\begin{equation}
\delta\langle K\rangle
=
\frac{\Omega_{d-2}\ell^d}{d^2-1}\,\delta\langle T_{00}\rangle+\delta X
\label{eq:conj}
\end{equation}
when the field is not conformal. He also states that finiteness
and universality of the UV area density $\eta$ ``remains an
assumption.''

Those sentences are the paper. They are not upgraded here.

The conformal half uses $K=H_\zeta=\int T^{ab}\zeta_b\,d\Sigma_a$,
the Casini--Huerta--Myers operator of a CFT. The installed
Standard Model has one-loop $b_3=-7\neq 0$ (Dynkin count,
auditor CONFIRMED). It is not a CFT. The conformal half of 2016
does not apply to NSM matter. \pP{}

\section{Why this is not a \pP{} substitute for Q2}

Paper~IX closes cosmology only if one has (i)~a family of de~Sitter
regions with controlled modular Hamiltonians, (ii)~a first-law
identity with a derived sign, (iii)~equivalence to linearized
Einstein$+\Lambda$. Torsion is not an item.

\begin{theorem}[not a substitute]
\label{thm:nosub}
Jacobson 1995 gives Einstein with $\Lambda$ free from Clausius and
an area law. That is not a derived cosmological first-law sign and
is not an FGHMV equivalence. Jacobson 2016 is not \pP{} for the
Standard Model. Therefore neither paper meets the Paper~IX bar.
Jacobson is not a \pP{} substitute for Q2. \pP{}
\end{theorem}

Local Rindler horizons exist at every point of de~Sitter. That
geometric fact is why 1995 can be \emph{stated} in dS. It does
not turn \eqref{eq:ein} into a derivation of the value of $\Lambda$
or of a holographic pair.
