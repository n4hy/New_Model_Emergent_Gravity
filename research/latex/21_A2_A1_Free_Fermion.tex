\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Jacobson 2016's nonconformal step is a conjecture:
$\delta\langle K\rangle=c\,\delta\langle T_{00}\rangle+\delta X$ with
$X$ a spacetime scalar. On a lattice the scalar is diagonal. The
local ansatz is therefore a \emph{tridiagonal} modular kernel.

A2, pre-registered: on a 1d staggered-mass fermion, vacuum interval
of $L=32$,
$R(m)=\lVert K-K_{\mathrm{tri}}\rVert_F/\lVert K\rVert_F$ obeys
$R(m)/R(0)<2$ for all $0<mL\le 8$. Measured ratios:
$1.07,1.16,1.32$ (solver) and $1.26,1.69$ (auditor, different
$N,L$). C2 PASS. The mutation (drop the diagonal) fires: a local
mass term is visible. \pP{} as a necessary-condition test on this
field, not as a 4d diamond theorem.

A1, pre-registered: the UV log coefficient
$\alpha=(S(2L)-S(L))/\log 2$ at $mL\le 0.5$ stays within $20\%$ of
$\alpha(0)=0.323\approx 1/3$. Measured relative drifts $0.021$ and
$0.072$. At $mL=6.4$, $\alpha$ collapses (mutation). \pP{} for this
1d free fermion. Not a 4d area law.

Road A may continue for this field class. It still does not give
FGHMV in de~Sitter or a value of $\Lambda$.
\end{abstract}

\section{A2}

$K=\log((1-C)/C)$ from the restricted correlator of the ground state
of $H_{i,i+1}=-1$, $H_{ii}=m(-1)^i$. Local ansatz $=$ diagonal $+$
nearest neighbour. Remainder of range $\ge 2$ cannot be a scalar
$X$.

C2 threshold was 2, locked before the run. The ratio grows. It does
not cross 2 inside the window. The conjecture is \emph{not refuted}
here. It is not proved in 4d.

\section{A1}

$\alpha(0)=0.323$. UV masses $mL\le 0.32$ shift $\alpha$ by at most
$7\%$. IR mass $mL=6.4$ kills the log ($\alpha=0.010$). Leading UV
piece is IR-mass independent for this fermion. \pP{}

\section{What this does not do}

It does not prove A2 for a 4d diamond or for the Standard Model.
It does not derive $\eta=1/4G$. It does not select $\Lambda$. It
does not produce FGHMV in de~Sitter.

\begin{admission}
A2 passed a necessary-condition test I could fail. A1 passed on the
same field. I will not write that Jacobson's conjecture is a theorem
in four dimensions.
\end{admission}
