\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Paper~25 found that the modular \emph{hop} tracks the CHM envelope.
Jacobson needs that envelope to do Clausius work:
$\delta S=\delta\langle K_{\mathrm{CHM}}\rangle$.

Two instruments were discarded before the gate was locked.
Bond-local $\chi=\Delta S/\Delta t$ versus $w$ fails the 1d theorem
($\rho\approx 0.28$): $\Delta C$ is nonlocal. A permutation of $w$
is not a mutation: the intercept of $K_{ij}=aw_{ij}+b$ dominates.

The effective test: fit $K_{\mathrm{CHM}}$ on vacuum nearest
neighbours, perturb every hop, and compare $\delta S$ to
$\mathrm{Tr}(K_{\mathrm{CHM}}\Delta C)$. Null: a \emph{flat} local
kernel, every hop equal to $\langle K_{\mathrm{NN}}\rangle$.
$\mathrm{Tr}(K_{\mathrm{exact}}\Delta C)$ is an identity and is
not a gate.

1d $L=24$: $\rho=0.918$ (instrument PASS). Auditor $L=20$:
$\rho=0.775$ (below the $0.80$ lock; QUALIFIED).
3d, all $282$ hops, $N=12$, $R=3$: $\rho=0.850$,
$R_{\mathrm{shape}}=0.527$. C1/C2 PASS.
C3 FAIL: the flat kernel is better ($\rho=0.912$,
$R_{\mathrm{shape}}=0.411$). Auditor $N=10$: same order
($0.797$ vs flat $0.865$). C3 CONFIRMED.

The first law tracks a local kernel. The CHM envelope is not
selected by Clausius. \pP{} as that negative. Not a refutation of
Paper~25, and not Einstein.
\end{abstract}

\section{Why these gates}

A2 and Paper~25 are about the shape of $K$. The next path step is
whether that shape is what generates $\delta S$. A test that a
shapeless local $K$ can win is the only mutation that can see the
difference. It won.
