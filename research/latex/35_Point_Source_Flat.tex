\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Paper~34 found a linear functional but could not name the
kernel: a Gaussian source makes CHM, linear, and flat almost
the same. This note puts $\varepsilon$ on a \emph{single site}.
Then $P_{\mathrm{flat}}$ is whether the source sits in the
ball, and $P_{\mathrm{CHM}}$ is the CHM weight at that site.

$512$ balls, $N=12$. $\delta S$ is linear in $\varepsilon$
(Pearson $1$). $\rho(\delta S,P_{\mathrm{flat}})=-0.829$.
$\rho(\delta S,P_{\mathrm{CHM}})=-0.465$. Residuals:
flat $0.559$, CHM $0.885$. C2 FAIL.

Auditor, $N=10$, source off-center: C2 REFUTED
($0.639$ vs $0.895$).

The linear bookkeeping is enclosed energy, not the boost
weight Einstein's first law needs. Not $8\pi G$. Not de~Sitter.
\end{abstract}
