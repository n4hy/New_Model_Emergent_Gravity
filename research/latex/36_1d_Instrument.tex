enclosed energy, not the CHM weight $R^2-r^2$. That is a 3d
statement only if the same operators recover CHM on a 1d
massless interval, where Casini--Huerta--Myers is a theorem

on one site. $\delta S$ is linear in $\varepsilon$ (Pearson $1$).
$\rho(\delta S,P_{\mathrm{CHM}})=-0.054$.
$\rho(\delta S,P_{\mathrm{flat}})=-0.276$.
Residuals: CHM $0.999$, flat $0.961$. C2 FAIL. C4 FAIL.

$\rho(\delta S,\mathrm{Tr}(K_{\mathrm{mid}}\Delta C))=1$

$\rho(\delta S,\mathrm{Tr}(K_{\mathrm{vac}}\Delta C))=-0.025$.

Auditor, $N=160$, $L=12$: C2 REFUTED; $K_{\mathrm{vac}}$
REFUTED; $K_{\mathrm{mid}}$ CONFIRMED.

Paper~35 is not a 3d no-go. The instrument tests a vacuum
first-law proxy that already fails the theorem case.
Not $8\pi G$. Not de~Sitter.
\end{abstract}

\section{What this is}

CHM still describes the \emph{shape} of $K$ (Paper~25).
This note asks whether $\delta S$ of a sliding interval,
after a point Hamiltonian source, equals a local functional
of $\delta e$. That is the Paper~35 comparison, moved to 1d.

It fails because $\delta S=\mathrm{Tr}(K_{\mathrm{mid}}\Delta C)$,
not $\mathrm{Tr}(K_{\mathrm{vac}}\Delta C)$. A point potential
is not a soft state variation at fixed modular Hamiltonian.
